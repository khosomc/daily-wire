UN Secretary-General António Guterres used his Islamabad visit to endorse Pakistan's mediation between Washington and Tehran, even as US Vice President JD Vance made a sharp cut in Iranian enrichment the price of ending the war. Pakistan tested an extended-range Fatah-4 cruise missile, the defence minister said Pakistani forces were giving reconnaissance support to Saudi operations against the Houthis, and Ishaq Dar warned that denying Pakistan its Indus waters would threaten regional security. At home, gunmen killed five people in Dera Ismail Khan as the government made further talks conditional on the PTI ending its march, while an IMF staff mission appeared set to clear about $1.2bn and the KSE-100 recovered strongly.

Geopolitics & Diplomacy

Guterres backs Pakistan's US–Iran mediation, urges return to diplomacy

At a press conference with Ishaq Dar in Islamabad, the UN Secretary-General praised Pakistan's central role in mediating between the US and Iran and called on both sides to end the conflict and restore freedom of navigation in the Gulf. President Zardari conferred the Nishan-e-Imtiaz on Guterres during the visit.

Vance says Iran must sharply cut enrichment to end war

In a Reuters interview carried by Business Recorder, US Vice President JD Vance said Tehran must make a meaningful reduction in its enrichment capacity to end the seven-month war, while admitting Washington was unsure how Iran reaches decisions in negotiations. The demand sets a high bar for the Pakistan-brokered track.

Conflict & Security

Gunmen kill five in two Dera Ismail Khan attacks

Unidentified attackers opened fire on a Federal Board of Revenue enforcement squad and, separately, on a highway traffic police vehicle in Dera Ismail Khan, killing five people, police said; outlets differ on how many of the dead were officials. Express Tribune also reported two militants killed in a gunfight in Bannu and three people abducted in Tank.
